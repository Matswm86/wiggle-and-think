# Brain & Body Activation for Kids (Ages 4-7): 20 Exercises, Science-Grounded, with Music

**Purpose:** Source document for a free, open educational website. Each exercise has a child-facing description (for animation/video), the honest science of what it does, an evidence grade, a matched music tempo (BPM) + free-music source, and a safety note.

**Date:** 2026-06-05. Compiled from 5 parallel research passes over peer-reviewed literature (PubMed/PMC, Frontiers, Sports Medicine, Child Development) plus OT/PE/yoga practice sources. Full citations at the end.

---

## READ THIS FIRST: What "brain activation" actually means (and what it does NOT)

The honest science is genuinely encouraging, but it does **not** match the marketing you'll find on most "brain training for kids" sites. To keep this website credible, we tell the truth:

### What IS supported by good evidence

1. **Cognitively-engaging movement beats simple cardio.** Movement that also demands attention, rule-following, memory, and decision-making improves executive function (focus, self-control, mental flexibility) more than plain running around. This is the single most robust finding (meta-analysis of 23 RCTs, n=2,857, ages 4-16: SMD 0.32; effect strongest for >2 sessions/week, >20 min). **Design implication: add a "thinking layer" to movement** (freeze on a cue, do the opposite, count while jumping, remember a sequence).
2. **Rhythm and beat-keeping help early literacy.** Preschoolers who can clap/march to a steady beat score higher on phonological awareness (the sound-skill that underlies reading) (n=156, ages 3-5, p<0.001). Clapping games, drumming, marching to a beat are well worth including.
3. **Jumping builds strong bones.** This is the *strongest* claim in the whole set: impact/jumping activities reliably increase bone density in children (12 high-quality RCTs). Real, measurable, lasting.
4. **Motor skill and balance have a small but real link to attention/learning** (meta-analysis, 37 studies: r 0.18). Small, but consistent across study types.
5. **Fine-motor / hand-eye (visuomotor) skill is the strongest predictor of math and reading readiness** (r 0.60 with math). If you can add hand-eye stations (catching, aiming, threading, tracing) as a bonus track, that's where the academic payoff is highest.
6. **Acute movement improves attention right afterward.** A 5-20 minute moderate movement burst measurably improves on-task behavior and attention for a window afterward. Best at moderate intensity (breathing faster but can still talk), done *before* a focused task.

### What is NOT supported (do not put these claims on the site)

- ❌ **"Cross-crawl / cross-lateral exercises connect the left and right brain" / "activate the corpus callosum."** This is **neuromythology**, traced to the discredited Brain Gym program. The corpus callosum develops prenatally and through adolescence on a genetic/developmental timetable; no study shows a movement exercise changes it. *Keep the exercises* (they're great coordination challenges) but **frame them as "both sides of the body working together," never as brain-hemisphere wiring.**
- ❌ **"Brain Gym" branding and its claims** ("brain buttons increase blood flow," "hook-ups move energy to the thinking brain," processed-food/water claims). Scientific consensus: pseudoscience. We use a few of the *movements* on legitimate coordination grounds, never the explanations.
- ❌ **"Left-brain / right-brain learner"** framing. Classic debunked neuromyth.
- ❌ **"This grows your child's hippocampus / raises BDNF."** True-ish in older children and adults; **not established in 4-7 year-olds** (the BDNF studies have no children under ~10). Don't claim it for this age group.
- ❌ Precise "spinning calms the nervous system for 2 hours" / "heavy work organizes the brain for 1-2 hours" dosage claims. Plausible, widely repeated by OT blogs, but **not** backed by controlled trials in typically-developing kids.

### The safe, honest umbrella message for the site

> "Active, playful movement helps young children feel calm, focused, and ready to learn, builds strong bodies and coordination, and is most powerful when it also makes them think, listen, and keep a beat."

Every claim in that sentence is defensible. Everything beyond it starts to drift into myth.

**A note on age:** "4-7" spans a big developmental gap. 4-year-olds often *cannot* yet reliably cross the midline, do a full jumping jack, or sync precisely to a beat. That's normal. Every exercise below has an easier entry version. For the youngest, music is for *pacing and fun*, not precise beat-matching.

## MUSIC: the system (use this for all 20)

Match the music tempo to the exercise's energy. These BPM bands are drawn from classroom-music research and validated against the tempos of real children's songs.

| Band | BPM | Use for | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Calm** | 55-75 | Breathing, balance holds, cool-down, stretches | Slow piano, soft strings, nature sounds, slow Baroque (Bach "Air" ~52, Pachelbel ~66) |
| **B. Steady** | 80-110 | Marching, walking patterns, coordination, clapping games | Clear march beat, folk, steady drum |
| **C. Moderate** | 100-120 | Animal walks, locomotor, gentle dance | Upbeat instrumental folk, light jazz, playful |
| **D. High energy** | 120-145 | Jumping, freeze dance, running in place, fast dance | Upbeat pop/electronic instrumental; stay **under 145** or it gets frantic |
| **E. Rhythm-led** | 80-116 | Drumming, body percussion (beat IS the task) | Simple, LOUD, unmistakable pulse |

**Rules that matter for ages 4-7:**
- **Instrumental (no lyrics) for any exercise where kids concentrate on moving.** Words compete with the movement task (a real cognitive interference effect). The exception: action songs where the lyric *is* the instruction ("put your right hand in").
- Strong, clear beat on every count; predictable 4/4 (or 3/4 for swaying/spinning). Avoid syncopation, EDM drops, distortion.
- Keep volume moderate (hearing safety + calmer kids).
- 1.5-3 min per track is the practical length.

**Free, legally-clean music sources (verified 2026-06-05):**
- **Pixabay Music** (pixabay.com/music) — royalty-free, **no attribution required**, website use explicitly allowed. *Easiest option.*
- **Incompetech / Kevin MacLeod** (incompetech.com) — CC BY 4.0, free incl. commercial, **must credit** ("Title" by Kevin MacLeod, incompetech.com, CC BY 4.0). BPM-filterable. Huge catalog.

## THE 20 EXERCISES

Grouped so the site can offer a balanced "full-body, full-brain" session. A good 12-15 min routine = 1 warm-up animal walk + 2-3 cognitively-engaging games + 1-2 jumps + 1 balance + 1 breathing cool-down.

---

### GROUP 1 — Animal Walks (strength, coordination, body awareness, proprioception)

#### 1. Bear Crawl
**Do it:** Bend forward, both hands flat on the floor, bottom up in the air. Walk forward moving your right hand + right foot together, then left hand + left foot. Keep knees off the floor. 5-10 steps forward, then backward.
**Activates:** Shoulders, arms, core, hips; whole-body coordination and "heavy work" joint input that helps kids feel calm and organized.
**Evidence:** MODERATE (as part of fundamental-movement-skill programs that improve coordination; the "calming" effect is plausible but not proven in typical kids).
**Music:** Band C (100-120 BPM). **Duration:** 1-2 min.
**Safety:** Clear ~3 m path. Slow and controlled, no racing. Wrist-sensitive kids can crawl on fists.
**Thinking layer (optional):** Call out a color/animal they must "walk to."

#### 2. Crab Walk
**Do it:** Sit, knees bent, feet flat. Hands on the floor *behind* you, fingers pointing toward your feet. Push hips up so your tummy is flat like a table. Walk sideways: right hand + right foot, then left hand + left foot. Keep the bottom up.
**Activates:** Triceps, core, glutes, shoulder stability.
**Evidence:** MODERATE (coordination/FMS).
**Music:** Band C. **Duration:** 1-2 min.
**Safety:** Look where you're going. Not on slippery floors. Stop if wrists ache.

#### 3. Inchworm
**Do it:** Stand, feet together. Bend and put both hands on the floor in front of your feet. Walk your hands forward until you're flat like a plank. Then keep hands still and walk your feet up close to your hands. Stand up. That's one. Repeat 4-6 times.
**Activates:** Core, shoulders, hamstrings, calves; full-body coordination.
**Evidence:** MODERATE (FMS/core).
**Music:** Band C, slower end (~100). **Duration:** 1-2 min.
**Safety:** "Keep your tummy tight" so the back doesn't sag. Own pace.

#### 4. Flamingo Hop
**Do it:** Stand tall, arms out like wings. Lift one foot, bend that knee. Hop forward 5 small light hops on the standing foot, then switch.
**Activates:** Single-leg balance, ankle stabilizers, hip strength, proprioception.
**Evidence:** MODERATE (balance is among the better-evidenced motor skills).
**Music:** Band C. **Duration:** 1 min (alternating).
**Safety:** Land soft on the ball of the foot. Wobbly kids touch a wall with one finger.

---

### GROUP 2 — Two-Sides-Together (bilateral coordination & crossing the midline)

> ⚠️ Frame all of these as **"both sides of your body working together"** and **"crossing your arm/leg over to the other side."** Never as brain-hemisphere wiring.

#### 5. Cross-Crawl March
**Do it:** Stand tall. Lift your **right** knee and at the same time bring your **left** elbow/hand across to tap it. Switch: left knee, right elbow across. Build a steady march. Aim for ~20 taps.
**Activates:** Bilateral coordination, midline crossing, core, motor planning. A real coordination challenge that demands attention.
**Evidence:** MODERATE as coordination/cognitively-engaging movement. ❌ NOT "hemisphere integration."
**Music:** Band B (80-110, marching beat). **Duration:** 1-2 min.
**Safety:** Start slow so they get the opposite-arm-to-knee pairing. 4-year-olds who can't cross yet just march in place: the crossing comes with practice.
**Thinking layer:** Speed up / slow down on your call; reverse direction.

#### 6. Windmill Toe-Touch
**Do it:** Feet wide, arms out in a big T. Swing your **right** hand down and across to touch your **left** foot (or ankle/shin). Back to T. Then **left** hand to **right** foot. 10 reps, alternating.
**Activates:** Midline crossing, core rotation, hamstring + shoulder flexibility.
**Evidence:** MODERATE (coordination/flexibility).
**Music:** Band B. **Duration:** 1-2 min.
**Safety:** Slow, no bouncing into the stretch. Dizzy-when-upside-down kids do a seated opposite-knee version.

#### 7. Lazy-8 Air Tracing  *(reframed as visual tracking, not "Brain Gym")*
**Do it:** Point one thumb up, arm straight out at eye level. Draw a big sideways figure-8 (a lying-down 8) in the air: from the middle, up-and-left, loop, back through middle, up-and-right, loop. **Follow your thumb with your eyes, keep your head still.** 5-8 loops each hand.
**Activates:** Visual tracking, eye-hand coordination, midline crossing. (Hand-eye/visuomotor skill is the strongest predictor of math/reading readiness, so this one quietly earns its place.)
**Evidence:** WEAK-MODERATE for visuomotor value. ❌ Drop all Brain Gym hemisphere claims.
**Music:** Band A-B (slow-steady). **Duration:** 1-2 min.
**Safety:** Keep the 8 *big* (shoulder-width) or the effect is lost. No risk; works seated too.

---

### GROUP 3 — Yoga & Balance (static balance, core, attention, calm)

#### 8. Tree Pose
**Do it:** Stand tall, eyes fixed on one spot on the wall (a "gaze point" helps balance). Shift weight to your left foot. Place your right sole against your inner ankle or **calf** (never the knee). Hands together at the chest or up like branches. Hold 3-5 breaths, switch.
**Activates:** Single-leg balance, ankle + core stability, focused attention.
**Evidence:** MODERATE (balance/attention link).
**Music:** Band A (55-75). **Duration:** 30-60 s/side.
**Safety:** Foot above or below the knee, never on it. Finger on a wall if needed.

#### 9. Airplane Pose
**Do it:** Stand tall, arms out like wings. Weight onto the left foot. Tip your chest forward and lift your right leg straight out behind you, until arms-body-leg are roughly level with the floor. Hold 3-5 s ("engine sounds!"), switch.
**Activates:** Single-leg balance, glutes, core, hip strength, focus.
**Evidence:** MODERATE.
**Music:** Band A. **Duration:** 30-60 s/side.
**Safety:** Move in slowly, no leg-swinging. Chair/wall support to start.

#### 10. Cat-Cow
**Do it:** Hands-and-knees "table" (wrists under shoulders, knees under hips). **Cow:** breathe in, drop your belly, look up, curve your back like a bowl ("moo"). **Cat:** breathe out, round your back up to the ceiling, tuck your chin. 5 slow rounds.
**Activates:** Spinal mobility, breath-movement coordination, core.
**Evidence:** MODERATE (mobility + breath regulation).
**Music:** Band A. **Duration:** ~1 min.
**Safety:** Wrists under shoulders, on a mat/carpet. Fists if wrists are sensitive.

#### 11. Downward Dog
**Do it:** From hands-and-knees, tuck toes, push your bottom up to make an upside-down V. Press hands down, reach heels toward the floor, let your head hang. Hold 3-5 breaths; "wag your tail" side to side.
**Activates:** Whole-body stretch, hamstrings, calves, shoulder strength.
**Evidence:** MODERATE.
**Music:** Band A. **Duration:** 30-45 s.
**Safety:** Soft elbows (not locked). Heels needn't reach the floor.

---

### GROUP 4 — Listen-and-Move Games (self-control, attention, the "thinking layer")

> These carry the **strongest** brain benefit because they pair movement with attention, rule-following, and impulse control.

#### 12. Freeze Dance
**Do it:** Music plays, everyone dances with their whole body. Music **stops**, everyone **freezes** like a statue and holds until it starts again. Variation: call a shape/animal to freeze into. Take one big breath before unfreezing.
**Activates:** Inhibitory control (stopping on cue), listening, aerobic movement, self-regulation. A textbook "cognitively-engaging" exercise.
**Evidence:** STRONG (concept): stop-signal games train the exact executive skill measured in the research.
**Music:** Band D (120-145), with clean hard stops (cut, don't fade). **Duration:** 3-5 min.
**Safety:** Mark a "safe dance zone," an arm's length each. Stop if kids run/collide.

#### 13. Simon Says — Coordination Edition
**Do it:** Leader gives a move only after "Simon says." Do it only if "Simon says" came first. Use midline + balance moves: "Simon says touch your right hand to your left knee," "Simon says stand on one leg," "Simon says make a star." Rotate the leader.
**Activates:** Inhibitory control, listening, body-part awareness, midline crossing.
**Evidence:** STRONG (concept) for executive function.
**Music:** Band B low, or none (verbal cues need to be heard). **Duration:** 3-5 min.
**Safety:** No jumping on hard floors; no eyes-closed moves in a crowd.

---

### GROUP 5 — Rhythm & Body Percussion (beat-keeping → early literacy)

#### 14. Clap Call-and-Response
**Do it:** Leader claps a short pattern (CLAP-CLAP-CLAP-rest); group echoes it exactly. Grow from 1-beat to 4-beat patterns mixing clap, thigh-pat, stomp, snap. Variation: half the group holds a steady beat while the other half claps a pattern over it.
**Activates:** Beat synchronization, auditory processing, bilateral coordination, listening. (Beat-keeping links to phonological awareness, the reading sound-skill.)
**Evidence:** MODERATE-STRONG for the rhythm-literacy association.
**Music:** Band E (80-116) or unaccompanied. **Duration:** 2-3 min.
**Safety:** On hard floors swap stomps for knee-pats (quieter for sensitive kids).

#### 15. Stomp-Clap-Pat Body Drum
**Do it:** Learn 3 sounds: STOMP (foot), CLAP (hands), PAT (thighs). Practice 4 of each. Then combine: STOMP-STOMP-CLAP-PAT, repeat ×4. Speed up, slow down, try eyes closed. Add a SNAP for those who can.
**Activates:** Rhythmic sequencing (working memory), bilateral coordination, auditory-motor integration.
**Evidence:** MODERATE.
**Music:** Band E. **Duration:** 2-3 min.
**Safety:** Knee-pats instead of stomps on hard floors.

---

### GROUP 6 — Jumps & Aerobic Bursts (bone strength + alertness)

#### 16. Kangaroo / Rocket Jumps
**Do it:** Feet together, hands like little paws at your chest, squat slightly. Jump straight up as high as you can, both feet together. Land **soft** with bent knees. Repeat right away. 8-10 jumps.
**Activates:** Explosive leg power, landing control; **builds bone density (strongest-evidenced benefit in the whole set).**
**Evidence:** STRONG for bone health; MODERATE for the alertness boost.
**Music:** Band D. **Duration:** 30-60 s.
**Safety:** Land on the balls of the feet, knees soft (never locked). Mats/soft floor; clear overhead space.

#### 17. Star Jumps
**Do it:** Feet together, knees soft. Jump up and throw both arms AND both legs out so your body makes a star/X in the air. Land soft, feet together, arms down. 8-12 jumps.
**Activates:** Whole-body explosive coordination, air awareness; bone-loading.
**Evidence:** STRONG (bone) / MODERATE (coordination). *Progression from jumping jacks.*
**Music:** Band D. **Duration:** 30-60 s.
**Safety:** Soft landings; ceiling height + clear floor. Use after kids can already do basic jumps.

#### 18. Jumping Jacks
**Do it:** Feet together, arms down. Jump feet apart to shoulder width while swinging arms up to clap overhead. Jump back together, arms down. 10-20 at medium pace.
**Activates:** Full-body bilateral coordination, aerobic warm-up.
**Evidence:** MODERATE.
**Music:** Band D, lower end (~120). **Duration:** 30-60 s.
**Safety:** ⚠️ **Developmental:** many 4-year-olds can't yet coordinate arms+legs together. Teach in 2 steps first (feet apart/together, then arms up/down) before combining. Soft landings.

---

### GROUP 7 — Dynamic Balance & Vestibular

#### 19. Tightrope Walk
**Do it:** Tape a straight line on the floor (or use a floor seam). Arms out like a tightrope walker. Walk heel-to-toe along the line, eyes looking **forward** (not at the feet). Try it backward; try a beanbag on the head.
**Activates:** Dynamic balance, vestibular system, heel-toe coordination, visual-spatial control.
**Evidence:** MODERATE.
**Music:** Band B (steady, slow). **Duration:** 1-2 min.
**Safety:** Flat, non-slip surface. Slow. Beanbag only after basic balance is steady.

*(Optional spinning note for the site: brief, child-controlled spinning, ~5-10 s, both directions, then stop, is normal healthy play. Stop immediately at any sign of pallor, glassy eyes, nausea, or sudden stillness. Not for children with seizure or known vestibular/heart conditions. We did not include a dedicated spinning exercise because the cognitive benefit isn't proven and the safety supervision is harder to convey in a video.)*

### GROUP 8 — Calm-Down & Breathing (regulation; always end here)

#### 20. Starfish Breathing  *(plus two alternates)*
**Do it (Starfish):** Spread one hand wide like a starfish. With the index finger of the other hand, trace **up** the outside of your thumb as you breathe **in**, and **down** the inside as you breathe **out**. Continue across all five fingers = five slow breaths.
**Alternate A — Balloon Belly:** One hand on chest, one on belly. Breathe in through the nose 4 counts (belly pushes out like a balloon, chest still), out through the mouth 4 counts.
**Alternate B — Rainbow Breath:** Breathe in while raising both arms in a big rainbow arc overhead; breathe out lowering them down the same arc. 4-5 times.
**Activates:** Diaphragmatic breathing, parasympathetic (calming) regulation, focused attention. The tracing/arc gives restless kids something to *do*, which makes breathing exercises actually work for this age.
**Evidence:** MODERATE (breathing for self-regulation is well-established practice).
**Music:** Band A (55-75). **Duration:** 1-2 min.
**Safety:** Never force deep breaths; if dizzy, return to normal breathing. Gentle tracing only. Perfect session-closer.

## How the 20 cover "all needed movement & brain/body activation"

| Domain | Covered by |
|---|---|
| Gross-motor / locomotor | 1,2,3,4,16,17,18 |
| Upper-body & core strength / "heavy work" (proprioception) | 1,2,3,11 |
| Bilateral coordination + crossing the midline | 5,6,7,13 |
| Hand-eye / visual tracking (visuomotor) | 7 (+ suggested bonus stations) |
| Static balance / vestibular | 4,8,9,19 |
| Dynamic balance | 19 |
| Spinal mobility & flexibility | 6,10,11 |
| Aerobic / cardiovascular | 12,16,17,18 |
| Bone-strengthening (impact) | 16,17,18 |
| Rhythm / auditory-motor / beat-keeping (→ literacy) | 14,15 |
| Executive function: inhibitory control, attention, working memory | 12,13,14,15 (and the "thinking layer" add-ons) |
| Breath / self-regulation / calm | 8,10,20 |

**Two recommendations to maximize the real brain payoff:**
1. **Add a "thinking layer" wherever possible** (freeze on a cue, do the opposite, count out loud, remember a sequence). The research is clear this is what separates ordinary exercise from executive-function gains.
2. **Consider a bonus "hand-eye" track** (catching a scarf, aiming a beanbag at a target, tracing/threading). Fine-motor/visuomotor skill has the *strongest* link to math and reading readiness, even though it's less video-friendly than the gross-motor moves above.

## Honesty checklist for the website copy

- ✅ Say: "helps kids feel calm, focused and ready to learn," "builds strong bodies, bones, and coordination," "moving to a beat helps early reading skills," "games that make kids stop, think, and listen build self-control."
- ❌ Don't say: "connects the brain hemispheres," "activates the corpus callosum," "left-brain/right-brain," "grows the hippocampus / boosts BDNF" (for this age), "rewires the brain," anything Brain-Gym-branded, or specific "calms for 2 hours" dosage promises.
- Add a short footer: *"Movement is great for every child. These activities are general wellbeing and fun, not medical or therapeutic treatment. Children with health, balance, or developmental conditions should check with a professional first."*
